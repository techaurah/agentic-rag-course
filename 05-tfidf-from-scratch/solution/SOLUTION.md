# Solution for Lesson 05: TF-IDF from scratch

Without stopwords, MRR falls the most: common words (the, and, is) enter every vector with low but nonzero weight, and their sheer count blurs the angle between question and chunk, so the right chunk stays in the top 5 (recall barely moves) but stops being first. Adding "fernhill" to the stopwords costs a little because the brand is not uniform: it clusters in the welcome, hiring, and guarantee documents, so it carried real, if weak, signal about which family a chunk belongs to. The general law: IDF already prices commonness; a stopword list is for words whose frequency is so extreme, or whose distribution is so uniform, that even a small weight times a huge count distorts angles. Both experiments are the same lesson at two doses.
